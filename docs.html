Spain is in launch certification. These docs describe the country contract we are promoting: authoritative cadastral identity, addresses, buildings, geometry, municipality routing, explicit territorial coverage, source provenance and strict separation of protected data.

Coverage contract

The Dirección General del Catastro is competent for 7,610 municipalities and its national cadastral system reports 78,941,430 real-estate records as of January 2026. The DGC territorial scope excludes País Vasco and Navarra, which maintain their own cadastral systems.

DGC common territoryCP / AD / BU and non-protected property-detail certification in progress.
País VascoÁlava, Bizkaia and Gipuzkoa require separate foral source integrations.
NavarraSeparate foral cadastral system; never substituted with DGC data.

Three separate signals

  • Match: what identity or geometry the request resolved to.
  • Coverage: whether the requested layer is promoted for that authority and municipality.
  • Provenance: which source family, municipality and source revision supports the returned fact.
No cross-authority fabrication. A DGC match must never be used to imply a property-level fact for Navarra or País Vasco. The correct behavior is authority routing or explicit unavailability.

Protected-data boundary

Spain’s Catastro distinguishes non-protected cadastral information from protected data. Publicly accessible non-protected information includes items such as property location, cadastral reference, surface, use/destination, cultivation/use class, construction quality and cadastral cartography.

Protected data include cadastral ownership identity and individualized cadastral value, including land and construction values. Those fields are not part of the open PropData Spain launch contract.

PropData launch rule: do not request, infer, reconstruct or expose protected ownership/value fields through the open Spain ingestion path. A future lawful source or separately entitled workflow would be a distinct product contract.

Geometry and coordinate resolution

DGC’s INSPIRE cadastral mapping publishes source geometry in ETRS89 / UTM zones used across Spain. PropData’s country normalization must preserve the source geometry contract while supporting WGS84 coordinate workflows at the API boundary.

  • Return authoritative parcel geometry only where the source layer has passed promotion gates.
  • Do not invent a polygon when only an address point or property identity exists.
  • Coordinate containment is a spatial match method, not proof of an exact postal address.
  • Keep source, municipality and revision evidence with downstream geometry.

Sources & licence

Dirección General del Catastro

DGC INSPIRE provides transformed datasets and services for Cadastral Parcels (CP), Addresses (AD) and Buildings (BU). Official ATOM download services and web map / feature services are the authoritative source basis for the common-territory graph.

Public-use licence

DGC’s cadastral-information licence permits public and commercial use for transformed value-added products. It does not authorize simply republishing or commercially redistributing the original supplied cadastral information unchanged. PropData’s normalization, deterministic joins, coverage semantics, source reconciliation and delivery layer are therefore part of the commercial product itself.
